# Tonight's family race

## Before the boys join — about five minutes
1. Keep all iPads and the Mac on the same Wi-Fi (not a guest network). Plug the Mac in.
2. Double-click **Play Tiny Racers.command** in this folder. Leave its Terminal
   window open. It opens the TV emulator and starts the game; music can play.
3. **To stop immediately:** double-click **Stop Tiny Racers.command**.
   Closing the play Terminal with Ctrl+C also stops the game/music.
4. Each new iPad needs the development certificate once. Open the setup address
   printed in Terminal (currently http://192.168.178.188:8081).
   Download the certificate; install it in Settings → General → VPN & Device
   Management, then enable trust in General → About → Certificate Trust Settings.
   Compare the fingerprint with Terminal. This is a temporary local demo setup;
   remove the profile after testing if the iPad won't be used again.
5. Scan the **current** TV QR with each iPad. Old saved room links expire after a
   new launch. Safari should open without a certificate warning.
6. Enable Rotation Lock on each iPad. The children can hold portrait or landscape.
   No synthetic opponent is started: you and the boys occupy real player slots.

## Tell the children only this
“Pick your character. Tap **Ready!**. If it asks to use motion, tap Allow.
Hold your steering wheel straight until the message disappears.”

Choose a map from its outline and 1, 2 or 3 laps before getting ready. Start with one lap.

Once everyone is ready, an adult taps **Start race**.
Turn gently left/right; acceleration and items happen automatically.

## Suggested first session — 15–20 minutes
- **Dining table:** one complete race with automatic items on and sensitivity 50%.
- **Garden:** let the boys choose. Start at the gentler 50% default.
  If it takes too much turning, try 60–70%.
- **Bedroom:** one race, then let one player try manual items in Pause options.
- Play again from the results via Pause/Options → Back to lobby; Ready calibrates
  each child's current grip again.
- The player who paused taps “Let’s go!” to resume. Other players see who paused.
  TV remote Menu can also resume. Lobby Menu opens music/effects sliders.

## What to watch, without coaching immediately
- Can each child get from choosing a racer to Ready with one instruction?
- Does steering turn the correct way? Options → Developer diagnostics → Invert
  steering is available if a device needs it.
- Does anyone fall off or get eliminated so often that it stops being fun?
- Are automatic weapons funny and understandable? Does the grabber feel unfair?
- Are ramps/boosters clear? Can everyone understand who won?
- Can all three finish a race and start another without you fixing anything?

## One adult-only recovery check
After a normal race, briefly switch Safari to another app and come back.
The affected iPad may need Connect → Ready again. Use the current QR if the
game was restarted. Confirm the other players/game didn't crash.

## If something goes wrong
- No motion prompt/data: use Safari, secure HTTPS link, keep the iPad upright,
  and retry Ready. If permission was denied, a parent may need to allow it in settings.
- Stuck “hold straight”: flat or upright screen, relaxed grip, no turning for two seconds.
- Can't connect: same Wi-Fi, Mac awake, Terminal open, current QR/token.
- Nobody can start: all connected players must tap Ready and be sending motion.
  Remove an unused controller through Options → Leave lobby.
- Music too loud: Stop command immediately; adjust sound from the TV lobby next time.
- Avoid fighting a recurring bug: note device/track/what happened and stop that test.

Record notes in submission/FAMILY_FEEDBACK.md. Short phrases are enough.
The Mac bridge and certificate setup are development requirements, not the final
consumer experience. Physical iPad permission/calibration is what tonight validates.

## Languages
Each controller starts in its device language, with an English fallback. Choose
Language above the join section to override it. Options → TV language changes
the shared TV display; it does not change other controllers’ language choices.
English, German, French, Spanish, Italian, Portuguese, Dutch and Polish are included.
Developer diagnostics, the development-certificate setup page and media credits
remain in English. This first translation pass still needs native-speaker review.
